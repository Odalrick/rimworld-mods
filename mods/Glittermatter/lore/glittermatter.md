# Glittermatter

The cheap plastic of the glitterworlds: a mass of programmable nanomachines that solidifies into almost any rigid
structure. On a glitterworld it is packing material. On the Rim it is a wall that goes up in an afternoon.

## The raw form

Raw glittermatter is stored as micromachines about the size of insects. That is a statement about size and nothing else
— they are not insects, and it is a loose band in any case, because insects run from barely visible to palm-sized, and
so do these.

The form varies as much as the size. A machine adapts to local conditions the same way the material it is made of does,
and the design library picks whatever suits the ground the machine will have to cross. Insect and spider shapes come up
constantly, for the same reason they do in nature: legs and wings cover nearly everything. Wheels are possible, and
occasionally chosen, but on anything short of a properly flat floor they are close to useless.

They are glittermatter all the way through. The machine is a format, not a shell with something else inside it — the
only alternative to a macroscopic format is a powder, and nobody wants to build a wall out of a powder.

Thousands of loose ones handle no better than that powder either, so they are configured to grip one another into blocks
of a size a person can pick up and stack. A block is not a solid; it is a crowd holding hands.

## Building with it

Building is not manual. The builder directs a block with a simple transmitter, and the machines let go of each other,
make their way to the work site — walking, mostly, and flying where the design runs to wings — climb up to where they
are needed, and dissolve into the structure. The energy that hardens them is energy the reactor put into them; the
builder supplies intent and nothing else.

This is the whole of glittermatter's advantage. It trades power and time for labour — a colony that can spare the watts
stops spending the hours.

## The glitter

The control is crude: one transmitter, thousands of machines, no judgement anywhere in the system. Not all of them make
it. The ones that run short of energy on the way fail to dissolve properly and set into the surface as they were — a
leg, a wing, an eye. The silver flecks in a dull grey wall are those.

It is where the glitter comes from, and probably why the stuff is called glittermatter. The competing theory is that it
is named after the glitterworlds. Either way, "glitterworlds" is the older word.

Most people find the remnants unpleasant to look at in detail. Builders who care will spend a little extra time making
sure nothing recognisable is left in a finished face — unless it is going to be painted anyway, or unless they simply do
not. What a later cut or breach exposes is nobody's problem in advance. None of it makes any difference to how well the
wall holds.

## Moving it and losing it

It is bulky for what it is worth, which is the reason nobody ships it. A trader will sell you a reactor and will not
take the output off your hands.

Every joule stored in the material is spent hardening it in place, so there is nothing left to reclaim from a wall once
it has set.
